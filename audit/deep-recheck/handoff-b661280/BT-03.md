## Acceptance criteria (for Deep to mark PASS / FAIL / BLOCKED with evidence)

- **orientation:** Each card is read with the meaning for its orientation (upright or reversed text from the card data below), and a reversed card is not merely the negation of its upright meaning.
- **position:** The position lens names this position and reads the card through the position’s defined meaning (see the table), without drifting in time or role.
- **neighbours:** The "wider spread" line relates the card to its pair partner (Celtic Cross) or neighbour (other spreads) without inventing a connection the data does not support.
- **clarifier1:** Clarifier 1 is read as modifying the original card ("how it modifies the original card"), naming both, not as a separate definition.
- **clarifier2:** Clarifier 2 develops the relationship between both earlier cards, naming all three.
- **synthesis:** "Together" is a synthesis of the cards, not a list; revisions are shown (Revision 1 kept readable when Clarifier 2 arrives; Revision 2 when the Clarity card arrives).
- **clarity:** Option B (approved 7 Nov): a Clarity card appears only when the original and both clarifiers are Major, is a Minor not already in the reading, and is read as grounding the three Major cards; a Minor original with two Major clarifiers gets none and the limit is stated.
- **max:** Max’s lines are the reading’s own words (quoted), in reading order; before Finish he invites and does not sign off; after Finish the closing comes once.
- **failure:** Deep’s six failure modes: templating, positional drift, reverse-as-opposite, parallel clarification, concatenation instead of synthesis, numerical/symbolic overreach.

### Card data each card was read from (data.js; provenance OPEN, see audit/sources/DATASET_NOTES.md)

- **Death** (ID 13; Major; number 13; element Water; astrology Scorpio)
  - Upright keywords: Transition, Release, Renewal. Upright: An ending or change of form asks you to release what has completed its purpose. Make room to grieve and attend to what remains alive; this card speaks symbolically of transition, not a prediction of physical death.
  - Reversed keywords: Clinging, Delayed Ending, Fear of Change. Reversed: Holding tightly to an exhausted arrangement may prolong uncertainty. Ask what you fear losing, then consider a smaller, supported act of release rather than forcing yourself to change all at once.
- **The Tower** (ID 16; Major; number 16; element Fire; astrology Mars)
  - Upright keywords: Disruption, Revelation, Rebuilding. Upright: A sudden change or unwelcome truth may expose a structure that could not hold. Attend first to immediate support and practical needs, then decide what deserves rebuilding on more honest foundations.
  - Reversed keywords: Avoidance, Instability, Internal Upheaval. Reversed: You may sense instability while trying to prevent any visible change. Take the warning signs seriously and make manageable repairs rather than waiting until the strain demands an emergency response.
- **Judgement** (ID 20; Major; number 20; element Fire; astrology Fire)
  - Upright keywords: Awakening, Review, Renewed Purpose. Upright: An honest review of the past can help you answer a new sense of purpose. Recognise what you have learned, make amends where possible, and choose the responsibility you are ready to accept now.
  - Reversed keywords: Self-Criticism, Avoidance, Unanswered Call. Reversed: Harsh self-criticism or fear of change may be drowning out a useful inner call. Distinguish responsibility from punishment and take one concrete step instead of waiting to feel fully worthy.
- **Two of Swords** (ID 51; Minor, Swords; number 2, Two; element Air; astrology Air · Swords)
  - Upright keywords: Deliberation, Stalemate, Boundaries. Upright: A decision may be held between competing needs. Protect enough quiet to hear both sides, but notice which missing fact or feeling must be acknowledged before a genuine choice is possible.
  - Reversed keywords: Overload, Avoided Decision, New Information. Reversed: Avoidance or too much information may be making the decision harder. Name the underlying concern and choose a practical next step rather than demanding perfect certainty.
